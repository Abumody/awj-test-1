📖 PASSAGE · LAKES AROUND THE WORLD

Water covers around 70% of the Earth's surface. The oceans contain most of the water. However, there are many lakes throughout the world. A lake is a large body of water surrounded by land. There are about 370 million natural lakes covering about 3% of the Earth's surface. Moreover, there are around 53 million man-made lakes in the world.

Most lakes contain freshwater and have at least one source, like a river or a stream. However, some lakes do not have a continuous source of water, and when the water evaporates, it becomes saltier. Fresh water lakes, such as Lake Victoria in Africa, contain less than 1 gramme of salt per litre of water. Whereas salt water lakes, such as the Great Salt Lake in the USA, may contain more than 250 grammes of salt per litre of water. Lake Assal, located in Djibouti, Africa is the second saltiest lake in the world after the Lake Don Juan Pond in Antarctica. Lake Assal is considered a "national treasure", and is used to produce salt and other minerals.

Natural lakes vary in terms of size, shape, depth, water chemistry and other features. For example, ‘Boiling Lake’ in the Dominica Republic, Central America, is considered the second largest hot-water lake in the world. The water temperature along its edges, is about 180°F to 197°F. It is impossible to measure the temperature at the centre of the lake, because it is too hot. Despite this, it has become very popular with tourists because it is covered in clouds, and its greyish water is always bubbling. Laguna Colorado lake in Bolivia, South America, is unusual because the colour of the water is red. It got its colour from a red plant that grows in the lake. It is a shallow lake with an average depth of only 0.35 metres. It attracts hundreds of migrant birds, especially flamingoes. 'Jellyfish Lake', located in Palau, The Philippines, is also popular with tourists who are interested in wildlife. It got its name because it contains millions of golden jellyfish. They are not harmful to humans, so it is a very popular site for snorkellers.

Man-made lakes are created by building dams to collect rainwater. These lakes are used for farming and for generating power. Lake Nasser, in Egypt, is one of the world’s largest man-made lakes. The lake is approximately 341 miles long and 22 miles wide. Most of the lake lies in Egypt, with a smaller part found in the Sudan. The Sudanese refer to this body of water as Lake Nubia. Similarly, Lake Kariba, located between Zambia and Zimbabwe, is the world’s largest man-made lake by volume. However, studies have shown that the pressure of such a large quantity of water has caused several earthquakes. To conclude, lakes provide an important source of water and income to countries and people all over the world and we must do everything to protect these valuable resources.
