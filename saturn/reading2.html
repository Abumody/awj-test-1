Dear Rose,

I would like to tell you about my latest trip to Madagascar which was from August 30th to September 4th, 2020. You may not realise that Madagascar is the fourth largest island in the world and is home to plants and animals found nowhere else on Earth.

My very uncomfortable flight involved flying from New York to London and then to Johannesburg and from there to Madagascar’s capital city, Antananarivo. It was exhausting and very costly so if you are planning to visit the island, take a direct flight to Cape Town and then to Madagascar which will only cost you $800.

I spent my first night in Antananarivo in an international hotel, though I would have much preferred to have stayed in a local hotel. The next day, I booked a guide with a mini-bus to drive me around the island and this was the best decision I made. Getting around the island is not easy as transportation services are poor and Madagascar has very few railways, because much of the middle of the island is mountainous. Most Malagasy people travel around the country in a shared taxi because it is cheaper.

My guide brought me to a beautiful rainforest where I saw the island's most famous animal, the lemur, a kind of monkey that only lives in Madagascar. Unlike the film Madagascar, you won’t see any tigers, giraffes or hippos. Luckily, the guide spoke excellent English and he told me that almost 90% of the plants and animals living on Madagascar are not found anywhere else in the world. He said that this is because of the strong ocean currents that have kept the island isolated from the African continent, so the species living there were unable to travel beyond the island’s borders.

Imagine the guide invited me to his home, where I had a traditional Malagasy meal called laoka. It was meat covered in a sauce that was flavoured with ginger, onion, garlic, and spices and served with rice. It was delicious, and tasted much better than the French meal I had eaten in the hotel on my first night. His wife told me that African and Asian ingredients are found in most dishes.

It was very interesting to see a country full of young people. Over 60% of the population are under the age of 25, with 30% aged between 25 and 60, and imagine only 9% are aged 60 and above!

You would just love Madagascar. Every evening, you can travel to different villages where you will see traditional dancing and musicians playing the valiha, which is similar to a guitar. It is considered to be the national instrument of Madagascar.

I spent my final day on the island whale-spotting. It was an incredible experience especially when one of the whales came close to our boat and showered us. We were all soaking wet after this experience.

Looking forward to hearing about your holiday,
Regards,
Maria
