The Sixth Sense: Can Animals Predict Disasters?

Are you aware of the sixth sense? Most of us know about the 5 common senses — taste, sight, smell, touch and hearing. But what about the sixth sense? It is basically the ability to notice something which isn’t there. For example, you feel like something is going to happen before even actually experiencing it. It is commonly known that animals have five main senses but is there really such a thing as having a sixth sense?

Sharks have long been thought to have a sixth sense. Most sharks have sensors on the sides of their heads, which they use to discover tiny muscle movements from animals nearby. Other animals including rats, chickens, and snakes have the ability to detect earthquakes! These animals have been known to leave earthquake zones up to days before the earthquake arrives. Scientists have long suspected that animals sense natural disasters sooner than humans do.

On December 26th, 2004 at Sri Lanka's Yala National Park, hundreds of tourists on the park's beaches were sunning themselves. But in the middle of giving their midmorning rides, the elephants ran up a hill, away from the ocean. Suddenly, a 30-foot wave — the Indian Ocean tsunami — crashed into the coast. The wall of water flooded much of the park, destroying buildings and killing almost four thousand people.

As the recent tsunami sped toward land, flamingos escaped and tigers rushed into their shelters. Even in the hardest hit areas of Southeast Asia, survivors found few dead animals. Many people wondered: Did animals know something we didn’t? "Some animals have a sharp sense of hearing and smell," says Alan Rabinowitz, of the Wildlife Conservation Society in New York City. "It allows them to sense something coming far before humans can." Vibrations from within the earth may have given the elephants in Sri Lanka a sign that disaster was about to strike. Indeed, a massive earthquake under the Indian Ocean, more than 700 miles away from Yala Park, caused the tsunami. Because the vibrations in the ground travel much faster than an ocean wave, the elephants may have felt the trouble before the tsunami crashed into the coast. But can the "sixth sense" of animals help us predict disasters?
