A World Memory Champion

Boris Konrad is a memory athlete. He has won more memory competitions than almost anyone else in the world. In the last ten years, he has broken over 40 national and world records. When he was a teenager, Boris read about people who could memorize whole decks of cards in minutes. He wanted to do that too. At first, he thought only geniuses could have such a powerful memory. He was an average student and felt his dream would never come true. However, later in life, Boris learned that memory is like a muscle — you can train it. He believed that nothing is impossible if you practise correctly. He tested this idea in 2015 by entering the German Memory Championship. Without any special training before that year, Boris finished second! After that, he started breaking records seriously.

First, he tried to memorize 300 random numbers in one hour. He failed at first, but remembering that 'anything is possible', he trained every day, used creative visualization, and tried again. This time, he memorized 302 numbers with only two mistakes and became the new national record holder. His achievement appeared in the 2017 National Memory Book of Records.

Today, Boris has a long list of records, including memorizing the order of ten shuffled decks of cards, recalling the names of 200 people after one meeting, and repeating a list of 500 random words without error. He says: "I choose challenges which are difficult and fun. I enjoy training and seeing my improvement." His favourite record was "memorizing the order of 10 shuffled decks" because it required total focus. He broke this record in 2019 during a live event. The most difficult record was memorizing 1,000 random objects in sequence. In 12 hours and 30 minutes, he reviewed the list 50 times and recalled 998 of them correctly.

If you want to improve your memory too, Boris gives this advice: "Choose a technique you enjoy because you will need to practise for weeks. Your mind will tell you that you cannot remember so many things, but that is not true. If you see someone doing an amazing memory task, try it and don't give up!"
