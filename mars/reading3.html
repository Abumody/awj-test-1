The Trans-Siberian Railway (TSR)

The Trans-Siberian Railway (TSR) in Russia, is the longest, continuous railway in the world. It is 10,000 kilometres long and covers one third of the globe. It runs from the beautiful city of Moscow in the west, to Vladivostok in the east, in Asia. It passes through 87 cities, five of which have a population of over 1 million people. The European part of the Trans-Siberian Railway covers about 19% of the total length while the remaining 81% is in Asia.

Construction of the railway began in 1891 and was eventually completed twenty-five years later in 1916. Projects are underway to expand the railway to Russia's neighbours, namely China, Mongolia, and North Korea. Most passengers travel from west to east. However, it is possible to go in the opposite direction. It takes about a week to complete the Trans-Siberian journey from Vladivostok to Moscow, the capital. There are many stops along the way, which last from a few minutes to half an hour. It has several sleeping cars which must be booked in advance. It also has a restaurant which serves three meals a day, bathrooms, and lockers. If passengers do not want to sleep on the train, they can arrange to stay in many of the Russian cities and towns along the way. They can rejoin the next available train later.

One of the most interesting sights on this trip, is Lake Baikal - the deepest and oldest freshwater lake on Earth. There are several routes to choose from. The most popular route is the Trans-Siberian Line. The second route is the Trans-Manchurian Line which runs from Moscow to Beijing, the capital of China. The third route, the Trans-Mongolian, goes from Moscow to Beijing through Ulan Bator, the capital of Mongolia. And the fourth route, known as the Baikal Amur Mainline, runs further to the north and reaches the Pacific Ocean.

The Trans-Siberian line remains the most important transport link within Russia; around 30% of Russian exports travel on this line. While it attracts foreign tourists, it is mostly used by Russians themselves. The regions served by the Trans-Siberian line possess more than 80% of the country's industrial potential and basic natural resources including oil, gas, coal, timber, precious metals, etc. The Trans-Siberian Railway carries about 200,000 containers per year to Europe. A trainload of containers can be taken from Beijing in China to Hamburg in Germany via the Trans-Mongolian and Trans-Siberian lines in as little as 15 days, but typical cargo transit times are usually longer and can take up to 25 days.

For those who love adventure, spectacular scenery, and getting to know people from around the world a trip aboard the Trans-Siberian Railway is a once in a lifetime experience!
