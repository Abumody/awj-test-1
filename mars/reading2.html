Dear Khalid,

I hope you are well. I promised I would inform you about the results of my research into the amount of plastic being used in supermarkets around the world. As we all know, plastic bottles are found everywhere, on our roads, beaches, and even in our beautiful wadis. Based on my research, I discovered that only 9% of plastic produced globally has ever been recycled. Fizzy drinks alone produced 90,000 tonnes of single-use plastic in 2021.

The environmental group, Greenpeace, produced a report in 2020 that demonstrates how plastic packaging could be reduced by at least 50% worldwide. Included in this report were actions that need to be taken to make this possible. For example, in future, customers could buy glass milk bottles and refill them directly in-store, and then wash them at home. In April 2020, Ocado, an online supermarket, stopped selling water in plastic bottles to reduce waste and free up space in their delivery vans during the COVID 19 pandemic.

Furthermore, there are lots of grocery shops in the UK that have refill dispensing machines for products, such as flour, rice, pasta, nuts, washing up liquid and shampoo. Shoppers can take their own containers or bags, fill them up at the dispensers and then pay for them by weight. In 2019, Waitrose, another supermarket, tried this scheme in their UK stores. It was a huge success and 90% of their customers said they wanted it to continue.

The data Greenpeace collected also showed that 20% of all the plastic sold in the UK is used to wrap fruit and vegetables. Supermarkets could save 30,000 tonnes of plastic by selling loose fruit and vegetables. Coconuts, for example, do not need to be wrapped in plastic! Apeel is an exciting new company that has created a formula made from plant waste which can be sprayed onto fruit and vegetables. The spray creates a layer which helps stop water loss, keeping the produce fresh for longer than normal. Carrefour, a French supermarket, has announced plans to make all their packaging and in-store plastic products 100% recyclable. A wide range of cheap and reusable alternatives such as cloth or paper bags will be available for customers as no plastic bags will be allowed in any Carrefour supermarket from 2024 onwards.

In Oman, plastic is one of the fastest growing pollutants and steps have been taken to prevent this, such as the ban on single-use plastic bags. The Environment Society, Oman (ESO) has launched a campaign called “It’s Time” to attract people’s attention to this very important issue.

Quite honestly, I believe we still have a long way to go before we stop using plastic. If we do not invent a plastic that can be recycled, we will continue using plastic for many more years. However, the good news is that we will be forced to use less because everyone is aware of the terrible consequences for our environment if we do not reduce the amount of plastic we use.

Please do write back and tell me about the research you are doing for your university in the UK.
Regards,
