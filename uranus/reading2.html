Dear Khalid,

I'm so excited that you've been accepted to join the 'Youth Scientists' team at the Russian research centre in Antarctica. It's not far from the New Zealand research centre where I work with scientists of different nationalities such as Chinese, Germans, French, Americans, Italians and many others. These scientists are working in various research areas. For example, there are meteorologists who are studying the effects of global warming, biologists who want to learn more about the plants and wild animals that live in this frozen land, and even psychologists who study how people behave when they live and work together in such a remote location.

My team consists of two researchers, and our manager is Professor John Smith. We have two main responsibilities. The first one is to learn more about the ice, as it is the oldest ice in the world. I believe the more we learn about the age of the ice, the better we can understand the Earth's weather. We also work as tourist guides to raise awareness about the importance of protecting Antarctica. Although this is the coldest continent, the buildings are heated to around 18°C, so we wear our normal clothes such as jeans and T-shirts. If I were you, I'd bring some. We only wear a thick freezer suit when working outside the station or meeting tourists. The station provides these.

Despite the unpredictable weather, tourism in Antarctica has become increasingly popular. The tourist season is from November until March. Last year, 20,000 tourists visited Antarctica. However, this season we are expecting more than 30,000 people, about 10 percent more than last season. Even though I appreciate a tourist's desire to experience this vast and beautiful landscape, I feel researchers should only visit Antarctica. Tourists make our work more difficult. For example, one of our main ice-preserving projects was damaged by a tourist. He bumped into a piece of equipment which delayed the completion of our work. Furthermore, according to the Greenpeace 2019 Report, tourists have destroyed the place by disturbing the plants and animals. As you know in Antarctica, it can take 100 years for a plant to grow back.

Since Antarctica is not ruled by any government, and there are no local residents, scientists from thirteen countries are working together to set rules and regulations to protect the environment by stopping tourism on this continent.

I look forward to meeting you when you arrive.
Best regards,
Ahmed
