📖 PASSAGE · READING 05

Dear David,

I hope you are well. I promised I would write and tell you about my most recent trip. Last week, I arrived in Tahiti which was a nineteen-and-a-half hour flight from Paris after a short stop in Sydney. I spent the night in a hotel close to the airport as I had to get up early the next day to catch my 7:40 am flight to Bora Bora.

Bora Bora is situated approximately 140 miles (225 kms) northwest of Tahiti and is a major tourist destination. I was met at the arrivals hall and taken by boat to the Sofitel Island Resort which is situated on a private island, and consists of a number of luxury huts that are located on the beach. It was just beautiful.

To get around Bora Bora, you need a boat as public transport is limited, consisting of a single bus that goes halfway around the island and back approximately every hour. Taxis are also available but are expensive. You can rent a bicycle or a scooter, but you have to go slowly as the road is narrow.

The first day on Bora Bora, I was exhausted after the long flight so I explored my beautiful resort. The next morning, I swam around the reefs for several hours. There were large numbers of beautiful multi-coloured fish. I then spent the afternoon enjoying the view, listening to my favourite music and looking out for black-tipped reef sharks. On the third day, I climbed Mount Otemanu which is 728 metres above sea level. It was a hard, tough climb but it was worth it as the views from the top were stunning.

On my last day, I went on a tour which included feeding sharks. One of the guides jumped into the water with a bucket full of fish. We also jumped in and within minutes, we were surrounded by some black-tipped reef sharks, some up to two metres long. I was surprised when one of them swam very close to me. It did this because I had cut my foot on the sharp coral, and the small amount of blood was enough to make a hungry shark want to eat me! Thankfully, the guide threw him some more fish to eat!

If you would like to visit Bora Bora, I suggest you come in the dry season from May to October. During these months the temperature is a few degrees lower and there is less rain. The water sometimes reaches 86F (30C), making it perfect for various activities, such as swimming, water-skiing, diving, snorkelling, etc. I do not advise you to visit during the winter months, in December and January, as it can be wet and windy. By the way, what I enjoyed most were the cultural events held each evening at the resort. There were also lots of interesting art galleries, studios, and shops that are set up in traditional and modern settings. I bought some beautiful black pearls for my mother.

I wanted to stay on this island forever, but unfortunately I had to return to Paris after the best four days of my life because I had to attend a conference. For me, it was a dream come true, and I can't wait to go back.

Hope to hear from you soon,
Paul
