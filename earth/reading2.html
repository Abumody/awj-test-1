I hope you are well. I would like to tell you about my school project on floating markets. Floating markets are found in places where boats are used to sell products like fruit, vegetable and handicrafts. These are run by local villagers. Today, most floating markets serve as tourist attractions providing travellers with an insight into a country’s culture. Thailand, Vietnam, China, and Indonesia are famous for these kinds of markets. Based on an international tourism report in 2018, the oldest and most famous floating market is Damnoen Saduak which is located about 100 kilometres southwest of Bangkok, Thailand. It was founded in 1866.

Damnoen Saduak Floating Market consists of three smaller markets: Ton Khem, Hia Kui, and Khun Phitak. Ton Khem is quite a large market. Hia Kui has souvenir shops on its canal banks which sell goods to tour groups led by guides. Khun Phitak is the smallest and least crowded of the markets. It is rare to find a foreigner or tourist there.

Each Damnoen Saduak Floating Market consists of a network of narrow canals. Female traders, wear traditional mo hom apparel (blue farmers' shirts) with wide-brimmed straw hats (ngob) and these images are regularly used to attract tourists to Thailand. They use small wooden boats called sampans to sell locally produced vegetables and colourful fruit. The market is generally very busy in the morning every day from 7 to 9 and it stays open until midday. A roof was installed so that it could operate in all kinds of weather throughout the year.

As you would expect, Damnoen Saduak Floating Market is full of foreign and local tourists. According to some tourists, traditional handicrafts are greatly overpriced. Bargaining is a common practice, although the prices of souvenirs and food are generally quite cheap. Cooks prepare traditional Thai food such as noodles, grilled fish and rice with coconut milk on their small boats.

According to the Tourism Authority of Thailand, the market attracts around 300,000 visitors during the weekends, on Saturdays and Sundays. Approximately, 180,000 of these are people from other countries and 120,000 are domestic. The market has also featured in several films. The 2008 film Bangkok Dangerous, starring Nicolas Cage, includes a scene that takes place at the market.
